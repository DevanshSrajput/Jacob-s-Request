An Assamese in Delhi: Failed Identifications, Ambient Racism on Grindr

June 13, 2021 · The Chinky Homo Project (curated by Kumam Davidson Singh)

An essay reflecting on identity, displacement, and the ambient racism encountered by people from Northeast India in mainland urban spaces — narrated through a Grindr interaction in Delhi. The piece examines how racial assumptions and failed identifications structure intimate digital encounters, tracing the gap between "passing" as a mainland Delhiite and the everyday violence endured by those who visibly "look Northeastern." It connects the politics of recognition on dating apps to larger questions of belonging, state-sanctioned gatekeeping, and the curious entitlement of the mainland "knower."

Research

My research sits at the intersections of queer anthropology, performance studies, South Asian studies, and digital ethnography. I am interested in how queertrans communities create worlds through embodied practice — in nightclubs, on sacred stages, during indigenous agrarian festivals, and across digital platforms.

Drawing on long-term fieldwork in Assam, India, my work explores how gender and sexual minorities navigate structural violence, precarity, and dispossession while simultaneously generating creative futures, new social possibilities, and rich aesthetic expressions. I combine traditional ethnographic methods — participant observation, in-depth interviews, and archival research — with multimodal and digital ethnographic approaches, including social media analysis, photography, and video.

Central to my scholarship is a commitment to understanding pleasure, joy, and play not merely as counterpoints to suffering but as political and analytical categories in their own right — modes through which marginalized communities assert presence, forge solidarity, and reimagine the conditions of their existence.

Current Projects

IMAGE

Dissertation: Queertrans Nightlife and Ritual in Assam

A multimodal ethnography examining how queertrans persons navigate and create community through nightlife in urban clubs, indigenous agrarian festivals such as Bihu, and sacred performance stages in the frontier region of Assam, India. The project traces how pleasure, precarity, and performance intertwine in spaces where belonging is continuously negotiated across lines of gender, sexuality, ethnicity, and caste.

IMAGE

Digital Queer Cultures of Northeast India

An ongoing digital ethnography exploring how queer and trans individuals from Northeast India use social media platforms, dating apps, and online zines to build community, share narratives, and resist ambient racism and heteronormativity across geographical and political borders.

Teaching

Teaching Philosophy

My teaching is grounded in queer, feminist, decolonial, and transnational perspectives. I am committed to centering voices and knowledge systems that have been historically marginalized — particularly those emerging from the Global South, Indigenous communities, and queertrans experiences — while challenging canonical, Western-centric approaches to anthropology, gender studies, and area studies.

In the classroom, I draw on ethnographic texts, critical theory, film, social media, and performance to create spaces for rigorous intellectual engagement and collective exploration. I believe teaching is itself a form of community-making: an opportunity to practice the careful listening, generous critique, and radical imagination that the worlds we study demand.
